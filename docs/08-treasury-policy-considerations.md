# Treasury policy considerations

Some Bitcoin-friendly businesses choose to keep part of their cash in Bitcoin. **This doc is a list of questions to work through with a CPA and an attorney, not a recommendation.** Holding Bitcoin is risky: its price can fall a lot, quickly, and a small business can run short of cash.

## Questions to settle before a business holds any Bitcoin
### Should we at all?
- What is our operating cash need for the next months (rent, payroll, loan payments)? What is left after a safety buffer?
- Who decides? What does our operating agreement or bylaws allow?
- If anyone else has put money into the business (co-owners, lenders, backers), what were they told, and what do our agreements and loan covenants allow? **Get an attorney's view first.**

### Policy options to discuss
| Topic | Options to discuss |
|---|---|
| **Source** | None; a fixed amount; a share of Lightning payments received; a share of surplus cash after a buffer |
| **Limits** | A maximum share of total cash; a minimum cash floor that always stays in local currency |
| **Custody** | Exchange custody; single-signature hardware wallet; **multisig** with signers in different places; a collaborative-custody service |
| **Signers** | Who holds keys; what happens if someone leaves, loses a key or dies; backups and recovery tests |
| **Selling** | When the business may sell (cash floor breached, planned expense), who approves |
| **Borrowing against Bitcoin** | Usually avoid in early stages; if considered, understand liquidation and margin-call risk and loan covenants |
| **Reporting** | Monthly holdings, cost basis, value, and who sees the report |

### Accounting and tax (CPA)
- How the business must account for Bitcoin it holds (rules differ by country and change over time).
- Cost basis tracking for every purchase and every Bitcoin payment received.
- Tax on gains or losses when Bitcoin is spent or sold; sales tax on sales paid in Bitcoin.
- Reporting obligations for prizes or payments made in Bitcoin.

### Legal (attorney)
- Disclosure to anyone with an interest in the business.
- Whether any public statement about the business holding Bitcoin could be read as promoting an investment.
- Money-transmitter or similar rules if the business ever holds or moves Bitcoin for others.

## Language to avoid in public
Don't market the business's Bitcoin holdings as a reason to join, sponsor or put money in. Avoid "returns," "yield," "growth of your money," or similar. The BitPadel mission is about **adoption and community**, not about anyone's financial gain.
